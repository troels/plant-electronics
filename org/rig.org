- *Host:* owns the experiment. It knows which jar is in which zone, which recipe each zone runs, and stores every reading with a timestamp.
- *Zone controller:* an ESP32-S3 per zone, since you already know the ESP32. It generates the light waveforms and the root current, reads the zone sensors, and reports over USB serial.
- *Why a microcontroller per zone:* pulse-frequency experiments need exact timing, which a Linux host cannot give directly.
- *Why USB and not Wi-Fi:* a cable gives power, a reliable link and no pairing. Wi-Fi stays available as a fallback.
- *Custom boards:* a zone-controller board and a current-source board are natural JLCPCB projects once the breadboard versions settle ([[file:shopping.org::#custom-boards][outline]]).

Alternatives to this layout:

| Layout                                  | For                              | Against                                  |
|-----------------------------------------+----------------------------------+------------------------------------------|
| ESP32-S3 per zone on USB (recommended)  | Exact timing, any host works     | Firmware to write                        |
| Raspberry Pi Pico 2 per zone on USB     | Same, slightly cheaper           | A second toolchain for you to learn      |
| Pi drives everything from its own pins  | Fewest parts, quick start        | Ties you to a Pi; weak timing; long wires |
| ESP32 nodes on Wi-Fi with ESPHome       | Almost no code, ready dashboards | Little control of timing and experiment records |

** Channels per zone

| Channel  | Wavelength  | Purpose                                 | Rough power |
|----------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+-------------|
| White    | broad       | Base growth light; also the photo light | 15-20 W     |
| Deep red | 660 nm      | Efficient growth light, spectrum ratio  | 10 W        |
| Blue     | 450 nm      | Redness and vitamin C lever             | 5-10 W      |
| UV-A     | 385-395 nm  | Redness lever, flavonols, tobacco       | 5-10 W      |
| Far-red  | 730 nm      | Optional; end-of-day experiments        | 5 W         |

The power figures are my estimates for a zone of about 30 x 45 cm at
200 umol/m2/s of base light and up to 60 umol/m2/s of blue.

** Two ways to build it

| Build                                         | For                                  | Against                             |
|-----------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| 24 V LED strips switched by MOSFETs (recommended) | Easy, cheap, pulses to tens of kHz | Wavelengths as sold; lower efficiency |
| 3 W star LEDs on constant-current drivers     | Exact wavelengths, efficient         | Heatsinks, more assembly; driver dimming range unverified |
| Addressable RGBW strip                        | One data wire, good for Stage 0      | Weak, wrong red and blue, fixed internal flicker |
| Commercial dimmable grow bars                 | No assembly                          | Little spectral control             |

** Dimming and pulsing are separate layers

- *Dimming:* fast PWM at 20 kHz or more sets intensity. At that rate the plant sees steady light.
- *Pulsing:* a slow on/off pattern on top of it, from 0.01 Hz to 1 kHz, is the experimental variable.
- *Why this matters:* ordinary PWM dimming is already pulsed light. If dimming and pulsing share one frequency, the pulse experiments are confounded.
- *Equal dose:* when a zone is pulsed at 50% duty, its intensity doubles so the daily light total matches the control.

** Calibration

Each channel in each zone gets a table of duty cycle against measured
light at plant height, made with a PAR meter. The per-zone light sensor
then watches for drift.

* Root-zone current
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: root-current
:END:

- *Circuit:* per jar, two inert electrodes in the solution and one channel of a programmable current source with polarity reversal. Voltage and current are logged.
- *Electrodes:* graphite rods. Copper, steel and aluminium dissolve; copper is toxic to roots.
- *Waveforms:* steady DC, slow pulses, and charge-balanced alternating current.
- *Range:* start in the microamp range and step up, watching pH and root colour. The published work says only "sub-lethal"; the level for lettuce has to be found.
- *Main confound:* current through nutrient solution changes the solution itself (pH shifts, reactive by-products). A control jar gets solution that was pre-treated with current while no plant was in it.
- *Fail-safe:* current drops to zero if the controller loses the host or the measured voltage leaves its limits.

** Camera protocol
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: camera-protocol
:END:

- Grow channels off, white channel on at a fixed level, fixed exposure and white balance, grey card in frame.
- Same times every day, at least twice.
- Per image: plant mask, redness index, greenness, projected leaf area.

* Software

- *Host services:* device manager (one serial link per controller), scheduler (recipes over time, per zone and jar), recorder, image pipeline, dashboard, optimiser.
- *Storage:* SQLite for experiment records (batches, plants, zones, recipes, assay results); Parquet for sensor time series; images on disk.
- *Protocol:* framed binary or line-based messages over USB serial, identical for real controllers and for the simulator.
- *Simulator:* virtual zones with virtual plants whose pigment and growth respond to the recipe. It lets the whole software chain run before any hardware exists.
- *Fail-safes:* a controller keeps running its last schedule from its own clock if the host goes away; UV has a hard daily limit in firmware; an over-temperature reading turns a zone off.

* Safety

- *UV-A:* enclose the shelf with a curtain and wear UV-blocking glasses when it is open with UV on.
- *Mains:* one enclosed 24 V supply on a residual-current-protected socket; everything on the shelf is low voltage.
- *Water:* electronics above or beside the jars, never below; drip loops on cables.
- *Root current:* low voltage, current-limited, floating supply. High currents make small amounts of gas at the electrodes, so keep currents low and the room ventilated.
