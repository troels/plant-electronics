* Anthocyanin in red lettuce
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: anthocyanin
:END:

- *Why first:* it is the only target with a published live, contact-free readout, and it has a lever that is known to work.
- *Readout:* the pigment absorbs green and reflects red. The index (red - green) / (red + green) from an ordinary colour camera tracked pigment content with R2 = 0.73 at canopy level in a 2023 study.
- *Known levers:*
  - Extra blue or UV-A light for the last six days raised redness in the variety Rouxai without reducing growth. More than 30 umol/m2/s of extra light was needed to see it. Blue and UV-A were about equal; blue is cheaper.
  - Applying the extra light early had no effect at harvest, so only the last week matters.
  - Cooling the root zone raised pigment in an older study.
  - Low nitrogen in the last week is commonly reported to raise it (memory).
- *Open:* pulsed light (studies disagree), root-zone current (untested).
- *Varieties:* Rouxai, Lollo Rosso, red oak leaf.
- *Faster alternative:* red radish or red cabbage microgreens finish in 7-14 days and carry the same pigment class (memory). Useful if a five-week lettuce cycle feels slow.

* Vitamin C in lettuce
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: vitamin-c
:END:

- *Readout:* vitamin C (ascorbic acid) oxidises readily at a carbon electrode. Voltammetry on plain screen-printed carbon electrodes has been used on fruit juice with almost no sample preparation, and on fresh vegetables.
- *Not live:* each reading needs a leaf sample of roughly half a gram (my estimate; lettuce is low in vitamin C, so a single small punch may be too little).
- *Known levers:*
  - Higher light intensity raises it, within a range.
  - Continuous light before harvest raised it; one study used 72 hours.
  - A higher blue fraction raised it in some studies; reviews call the effect of spectrum uncertain.
  - High intensity plus high blue also causes oxidative stress, so there is an optimum.
  - A 2025 study tested light/dark alternation at different frequencies in the last days before harvest, which is close to your interest in frequency.
- *Practical traps (memory):* vitamin C degrades within minutes once tissue is crushed, and it varies over the day. Sample at a fixed hour, extract in cold acid, measure immediately.

* Other light-absorbing compounds in the same plants
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pigments
:END:

- *Chlorophyll:* greenness from the same camera images, or from a spectral sensor.
- *Flavonols:* colourless UV-A absorbers in the leaf skin. The established method excites chlorophyll fluorescence once with UV-A and once with red light; the flavonols block the UV beam but not the red one, so the ratio gives their amount. Commercial clips (Dualex) do this. A home-built version needs two LEDs, a filtered photodiode and calibration against extracts. Stretch goal.
- *Carotenoids:* hard to separate from chlorophyll by camera; needs an extract and a photometer (memory).

Candidates outside lettuce, not planned, all from memory:

| Compound        | Plant                  | Note                                         |
|-----------------+------------------------+----------------------------------------------|
| Betalains       | Red chard, amaranth    | Red pigment, camera-readable like anthocyanin |
| Aroma compounds | Basil, mint            | Readable in air with gas sensors, non-specific |
| Hypericin       | St John's wort         | Red photosensitiser; slow plant              |
| Furanocoumarins | Parsnip, giant hogweed | Phototoxic on skin; avoid                    |

* Nicotine in tobacco
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: nicotine
:END:

- *Biology:* made in the roots, shipped to the leaves. Leaf wounding raises jasmonate in the leaf within 90 minutes and in the roots about 3 hours later; leaf nicotine is higher five days on.
- *Known levers:*
  - Wounding and topping (cutting off the growing tip) raise it.
  - Methyl jasmonate added to the roots of hydroponic plants raises it.
  - Ethylene suppresses the rise.
  - Near-UV light added to visible light raised alkaloids in expanding leaves in a 1973 whole-plant study.
  - Plants given red light last each day had more alkaloid than plants given far-red last (1970).
- *Electrical route:* untested. A leaf-puncture experiment suggests the leaf-to-root message is slow and chemical, so stimulating a leaf is the weaker bet. Current through the root zone is the better bet, because sub-lethal current triggered defence compounds in the roots of other plants.
- *Readout:* voltammetry on a leaf-punch extract. Nicotine oxidises at about +0.6 to +1.3 V depending on the electrode. Related alkaloids will probably appear in the same peak (my inference).
- *Plant choice (memory):* common tobacco used young, at the rosette stage, or the smaller lab species /Nicotiana benthamiana/.
- *Safety:* see [[file:assays.org::#safety][assay safety]].
